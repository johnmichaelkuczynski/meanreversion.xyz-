---
name: Landing chart priority
description: Mean Reversion owns page 1; all SOXL content belongs on separate page 2.
---

Mean Reversion owns page 1 and is the default landing page, including its score area
and a large chart visible even before a ticker is entered. Keep its original
ticker entry below that chart and a duplicate at the top of the page.
All existing SOXL/SRH content belongs on a separate
page 2, not below Mean Reversion on the same page.

**Why:** The user explicitly required separate pages for Mean Reversion and
SOXL, superseding both the SOXL-first order and the combined-page layout.
The user subsequently requested an exact duplicate of the ticker form at the
top so visitors need not search for the original.

**How to apply:** Do not show the SOXL account header, quote, diagnostics or
signal on the Mean Reversion page. Within the SOXL page, preserve
the daily signal followed by its actual interactive drawing/projection chart.

Page 1 must open with SOXL already plotted, its three trend lines and scores
visible, before the visitor enters a ticker. Entering another ticker replaces SOXL.

**Why:** The user repeated that a blank starting chart gives visitors no idea
what the function does; SOXL is the required illustrative default.

**How to apply:** Do not treat a failed initial history request as a completed
initialization. Preserve deliberate removal, but allow the SOXL example to be restored.

Put the active ticker and company name, WINNER / NOT A WINNER, whether mean
reverting, whether the mean rises, annual slope, and dated above/below-mean
distance at the absolute top in large black letters, one fact per line.
Keep an obvious primary ticker input at the top and its duplicate below the chart.
Do not displace these facts with a large generic app name, tagline, or blank space.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected paragraphs and buried facts, asking for
"ONE LINE; ONE DATUM" so visitors need not search.
The user repeatedly required the winner verdict and key stock facts at the very
top, then explicitly requested big black letters and rejected the generic app
name displacing them.

**How to apply:** Both ticker forms must replace the same primary stock and
update the heading and facts together. Label the actual price-observation date.

Put the current stock price directly underneath the ticker in large, but not huge,
text.

**Why:** The user said the current price was very hard to find and explicitly
required this placement.

**How to apply:** Preserve this prominent price line when changing the headline
facts; keep its observation time visible.

Make the stock's daily gain or loss prominent at or near the top of the Mean
Reversion readout, including the last trading day's change when markets are closed.

**Why:** The user said even a roughly 30% SOXL daily move was nowhere to be found.

**How to apply:** Keep the dated daily change beside the headline price, rather
than burying it in a chart hover or supporting details.

Use three fixed, straight lines on the Mean Reversion log-price chart, like
the SOXL channel, not bent rolling bands. Let the user extend them at least
30 years and 100 years ahead. The displayed slope and above/below-mean distance
must describe the same fixed mean that is drawn.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected bent lines that conform to price and
repeated the requirement for straight lines and long user-controlled extension.

**How to apply:** The subsequent explicit scoring correction requires a
seven-year recency-weighted straight mean for both scoring and display.
Follow the winner scoring contract; do not restore unweighted chart OLS
or rolling recovery scoring.

The Mean Reversion tricolor readout must be large and readable, like SOXL's,
with explicit Price and Probability per line and Expand/Restore, Collapse/Show,
and resize controls.

**Why:** The user repeatedly rejected the tiny repeated native hover tooltip
and explicitly requested all three size/visibility controls.
They again rejected a panel covering the chart and repeated that it must be
"resizable, collapsible, stretchable, movable".

**How to apply:** Keep one shared-date readout for all three lines, and keep it
reachable when the pointer moves from a line to its controls. Do not cover the
chart by default. Make move, shrink, stretch, and collapse actions obvious and
usable in a narrow embedded preview as well as a wide browser.
Fit the docked readout to its contents rather than leaving a large empty black
rectangle beneath its rows. Extra panel area should result only from deliberate
user stretching.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected the panel's dead empty black space.

Chart controls must start expanded and readable, with the header fully below the incline bar. Do not auto-shrink the panel to the small portion of the iframe visible when it first enters the viewport.

**Why:** The user rejected the clipped header and compressed initial panel as unrecognizable. Allow deliberate user resizing/collapse, but not automatic miniature controls.

Keep signal cards compact, with large signal words and readable plain-English
explanations. Keep validation statistics, sample-size warnings, range metadata
and scale legends in collapsed details rather than tiny footnotes beneath BUY.

**Why:** The user repeatedly said that technical text beneath NO EDGE belongs
in the dropdown and explicitly asked to remove the micro-font beneath BUY.

Put maintenance information about background updates and saved data at the very
bottom of the page, collapsed, not above the investor-facing signals or chart.

**Why:** The user explicitly said it belongs "way at the bottom" so it does not
distract people deciding whether to buy SOXL.

Never show colored warning panels or boilerplate financial/legal disclaimers
in the app or newly generated reports. A missing price should have no warning
banner or setup commentary. Preserve plain operational feedback and timestamps.

**Why:** The user repeatedly demanded removal and explicitly said:
"No colored warnings. No disclaimers. Ever."

**How to apply:** Keep notifications neutral, omit quote-unavailability notices
and boilerplate disclaimer blocks, and do not change prices or calculations.

Put the custom-stock ticker input in the console's first content area, formerly
occupied by the CHART CONTROLS heading; in a wide console it sits beside benchmarks.

**Why:** The user explicitly pointed to that blank area as the intended input
location after being unable to find it among the benchmark controls.
